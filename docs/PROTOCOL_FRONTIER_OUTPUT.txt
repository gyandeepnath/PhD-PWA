PROTOCOL FRONTIER — what each way of shortening a sitting costs
================================================================================================================
  2000 simulated participants per variant, identical participant stream across variants.
  Power assumes n=130, true d_z=0.3, between-participant SD of the difference score=0.1.
  Every participant must accumulate 20 condition-runs (10 conditions x 2 illumination levels).
  "contact" is median sitting length x sittings: the participant's total time in the lab.

  The power columns are the PRIMARY outcome only — the incomplete-blink ratio. They are
  blind to what a shorter go/no-go block costs, so d' SE is reported alongside them: it is
  the per-participant, per-condition standard error of d-prime at a hit rate of .95 and a
  false-alarm rate of .10. A variant that leaves the power columns untouched but raises
  d' SE is NOT free — it has moved the cost onto a secondary outcome.

THE FIXED-OVERHEAD WALL — why 20 minutes is not a matter of trimming
================================================================================================================
  Minutes a sitting costs BEFORE the first condition runs:

    overhead variant                first visit  repeat visit
    as shipped                         22.0 min      16.0 min
    CVS-Q end only                     18.5 min      12.8 min
    CVS-Q once per participant         15.6 min      10.1 min

  Marginal cost of one further condition: 7.14 min.
  So a sitting costs   T(k) = overhead + 7.14 x k.
    with "as shipped" overhead (16.0 min on a repeat visit), a 20-minute sitting affords 0 conditions.
    with "CVS-Q end only" overhead (12.8 min on a repeat visit), a 20-minute sitting affords 1 condition.
    with "CVS-Q once per participant" overhead (10.1 min on a repeat visit), a 20-minute sitting affords 1 condition.

  Read that last block carefully: the overhead alone is the binding constraint, not the
  conditions. Reaching 20 minutes requires cutting what happens AROUND the conditions
  first, and only then how many of them there are.

================================================================================================================
ATTRITION — the reason a shorter sitting can cost you MORE participants, not fewer
================================================================================================================
  A shorter sitting is bought with more visits, and every visit after the first is an
  opportunity to not come back. If the per-return dropout probability is q, then a design
  requiring v visits retains (1-q)^(v-1) of those who started.

  q is NOT measured here — it is a parameter, swept across three values. Substitute a
  real figure from your own pilot or from the literature before quoting any of this.

     visits   sitting  contact   retained @q=5%  retained @q=10%  retained @q=15%
    -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
          2       93m     187m              95%              90%              85%
          4       56m     223m              86%              73%              61%
          5       41m     207m              81%              66%              52%
         10       26m     260m              63%              39%              23%

    To ANALYSE n=130 you must RECRUIT n / retention:

================================================================================================================
WHAT THE TABLE ACTUALLY SAYS
================================================================================================================
  1. Cutting CONDITIONS PER SITTING costs no statistical power at all — every variant above
     holds the same power, because total condition-runs are conserved at 20 per
     participant however they are packaged. What it costs is VISITS, and therefore total
     contact time (which goes UP, because the fixed overhead is paid once per visit) and
     retention (which goes DOWN, because every return is a chance to not return).

  2. Cutting READING EXPOSURE is the only lever that reduces total contact time, and it is
     the only one that costs power. It costs it unevenly: the polarity main effect barely
     moves, while the polarity x colour interaction — the contrast the study is actually
     about — falls fastest, because it contrasts single conditions instead of averaging.

  3. The interaction is ALREADY below the synopsis target at full exposure. That is a
     pre-existing problem this table did not create, and it is the reason exposure is the
     most expensive thing in the protocol to cut.

  4. A 20-minute sitting is not reachable by trimming: the fixed overhead alone exceeds it
     on a first visit and nearly exhausts it on a repeat. It becomes reachable only by
     moving the CVS-Q out of the sitting AND running 1-2 conditions per visit AND
     truncating the passages — which together turn 2 visits of ~93 min into 10 visits of
     ~22 min, with MORE total contact time and much worse retention.
